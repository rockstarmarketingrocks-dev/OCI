6. Content rewrites (paste-ready)

H2: Crab Island Through the Day (replaces both "Looks Like" sections)

Crab Island changes by the hour. Around 8 a.m. the water is glassy, the sand shows clearly through three feet of green, and only a few boats are anchored. From about 10 to noon the rental pontoons and vendor boats arrive and it feels lively without being packed.

Noon to 4 p.m. is the scene you see in photos: hundreds of boats rafted together, music from every direction and paddleboards weaving between hulls. The water gets cloudier from all the movement. After 4 or 5, rental boats head back, the light gets better and the crowd thins out. For the best hours by season, see the best time to visit Crab Island.

H2: What to Do at Crab Island (split list into H3s)

H3: Floating Food and Drink Vendors
Vendor boats sell ice cream, boiled peanuts, tacos and cold drinks in the warm months. Bring cash. See what to bring to Crab Island.

H3: Paddleboarding and Kayaking
The flats are calm enough to paddle between boats. Watch for anchor lines.

H3: Dolphin Watching
Dolphins are often seen in the pass and the bay nearby, especially toward evening. A private sunset cruise is built around that time of day.

H3: Parties and Celebrations
Birthdays, reunions and bachelorette parties raft up at the sandbar all season.

New FAQ

Can you walk to Crab Island? No. There is no bridge, path or beach access. You can only reach it by boat, kayak or paddleboard, and paddling across the channel near the Marler Bridge is risky because of boat traffic and current. See how to get to Crab Island.
