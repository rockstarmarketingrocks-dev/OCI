5. Content rewrites & additions

(Intro, replace "Short answer: yes…" with:) Short answer: yes, Crab Island is kid-friendly when you go with a plan. The water is mostly 1–4 feet deep and calm, and on a captained tour you get lifejackets, a restroom and someone watching the weather. Like any open water, it has real risks: boat traffic, the current in the nearby channel, sun and crowds. Here's what to know so your family has a safe, fun day.

H2Real Risks at Crab Island (and How to Avoid Them)

H3Strong current in the channel

The sandbar is shallow, but the East Pass channel beside it is deep with a strong tidal current. Keep kids on the sandbar side of the boat and never let anyone swim toward the channel or the bridge.

H3Boat traffic

Hundreds of boats come and go on busy days. Set a clear boundary around your boat and keep little ones away from where boats are anchoring or leaving.

H3Changing depth with the tide

Water that's knee-deep at low tide can be chest-deep a few hours later, and there are drop-offs at the edges of the sandbar. Lifejackets on non-swimmers at all times.

H3Sun and heat

Reflection off the water doubles your exposure. Reapply sunscreen every 80 minutes, keep kids drinking water and take shade breaks on the boat.

H3Afternoon storms

Summer thunderstorms can build quickly over the Gulf. Our captain watches radar and heads in early if lightning threatens, and we'll contact you about any changes. [Owner: link the weather/cancellation policy.]

H3Alcohol around the sandbar

Crab Island has a party side, especially on summer weekends. Mornings and weekdays are calmer, and with a captain driving your boat, nobody in your group needs to drive after drinking.
